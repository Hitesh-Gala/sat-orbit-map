NAZAR

Nonchalantly Assembled Zero-Budget Assessment of Chinese Reconnaissance

Kyon ki NAZAR lagni chahiye!

A free, open, browser-only satellite tracker that turns NORAD orbital data into globes, maps, and click-through stories. Built at the Takshashila Institution.

▶ Launch the live globe How to use it

1 · Why this exists

Orbital reconnaissance shapes modern statecraft, but the data describing it lives mostly in spreadsheets, NORAD JSON dumps, and PDFs that are useless to a casual reader. NAZAR translates that data into globes, maps, and graphs so anyone with a laptop can ask questions like:

  • Which Chinese satellites are overhead from New Delhi right now?
  • Where will Yaogan-37 be in 30 minutes?
  • What does the world's GEO ring look like from above?
  • Which country owns the most active payloads in LEO?
  • Can a sensor on a particular satellite see my exact location?

…without ever leaving the browser. There is no backend, no account, no telemetry, no API keys you need to sign up for. Every satellite is propagated locally in your browser using the same NORAD/CelesTrak TLE data the rest of the world uses.

The acronym hints at the focus — Chinese spacecraft visibility — but the underlying machinery works for every sat in the catalogue.

2 · What it can do

🛰
Track ~16,000 active satellites
Every active object in CelesTrak's catalogue, propagated locally in your browser using SGP4 — the same algorithm NORAD uses.
🌍
Country attribution
Owner code, launch country, and physical launch site (Baikonur, Cape Canaveral, Kourou…) joined in from CelesTrak's SATCAT.
📚
Cumulative database
SatStats remembers every sat you've ever seen, so the table grows over time as new launches enter the catalogue — 19,000+ records and climbing.
📐
Geometry puzzles
Build cones from the ground up or from satellites down. Find which sats are visible to a sensor, or which patches of Earth a satellite can image.
🌐
Full 3-D visualisation
Every active sat at its real altitude — the LEO swarm, MEO band, and GEO ring as distinct radial layers around Earth.
🔁
Auto-refresh data
A scheduled GitHub Actions workflow re-pulls CelesTrak every 6 hours and commits the fresh snapshot to the repo — your offline fallback is never more than a few hours stale.

3 · The cool stuff

  • Click any satellite on the main globe and the rest of the catalogue fades to background while a tube shows the sat's past 30 minutes of ground track, capped by an arrow pointing where it's heading next.
  • The 3-D Visualiser drops every active sat at its real altitude. Toggle LEO / MEO / GEO / HEO independently to see the structure of the constellation — the GEO ring is genuinely a ring.
  • Game of Cones is two interactive geometry puzzles: a ground cone that finds sats inside, and a satellite cone that projects its sensor footprint onto Earth as a polygon.
  • The Orbit Visualisation page plays the NAZAR soundtrack with beat-driven camera moves — orbital pop-and-lock.
  • SatStats has a country filter: pick a country from the dropdown above the "Country of Origin" column and the table collapses to just that country's sats. The Launch Country column shows both country and physical site name (e.g. United States / Cape Canaveral).
  • The bundled data falls back gracefully: when CelesTrak rate-limits your IP (HTTP 403, which happens often), every page silently switches to the auto-refreshed snapshot in data/. You'd never notice.
  • Mobile users get a unified ☰ MENU drop-down that collapses every panel and link into one drawer, so the globe stays the dominant view.

5 · How to use the site

Navigating between pages

  • The left-side nav bar (⊞ 2D View, ↻ Orbit Visualisation, ◭ Game of Cones, ★ China Sat Repo, ⬢ 3D Visualiser, ⊕ SATs-by-OPs) is the primary navigation on the main page.
  • The top-right Takshashila logo opens takshashila.org.in in a new tab.
  • On mobile (≤ 720 px) all that chrome collapses into a single ☰ MENU button at the top-left — tap it to see the drawer.

Driving the globe

  • Drag to rotate. Scroll / pinch to zoom. Right-drag to pan.
  • Hover any sat dot for an instant tooltip (name, altitude, sub-point, azimuth / elevation).
  • Click a sat to isolate it — past 30-min ground track + direction arrow appear, the other 119 dots fade out.
  • Click the same sat again, or anywhere on empty globe, to deselect everything.

The right-side HUD on the main page

  • The UTC / IST panel shows live clocks in both time zones.
  • Total SVs Tracked is the size of the live catalogue.
  • Over Horizon Non-Chinese SVs and Over Horizon Chinese SVs are collapsible — click the summary line to expand the list of every sat currently above the New Delhi horizon, sorted by elevation.
  • Custom Location takes any lat/lon and recomputes the over-horizon list from that point. Use it to check what's overhead from anywhere on Earth.
  • The big ▦ SAT-STATS button below the clocks jumps to the cumulative database page.

Using SatStats

  • The big table lists every satellite the cumulative database has ever seen.
  • Above the Country of Origin column there's a dropdown — pick a country to filter the table.
  • The Launch Country cells show both country and the physical launch-site name (e.g. United States / Cape Canaveral).
  • The Photo column carries a thumbnail linking to an open-source image of the satellite or its series.
  • Switch to the Graphs tab for five distribution charts: orbit class, top owners, launches per year, launch sites, inclination histogram.

Playing Game of Cones

  • Land Cone mode: enter your lat/lon, set the half-angle and max height with the sliders. Every sat inside that upward cone appears in the list and on the globe.
  • Sat Cone mode: search for a satellite, set the sensor half-angle. The cone projects onto Earth's surface as an orange polygon — that's the area the sensor can see.
