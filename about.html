What is this?

NAZAR is a live satellite tracker. It follows close to 16,000 satellites in real time and turns the raw NORAD orbital data into globes, maps and graphs you can actually play with. Everything runs inside your browser — there is no backend, no account, no tracking, and no API key to sign up for.

The acronym hints at the original focus — Chinese spacecraft visibility over India — but the machinery works for every satellite in the catalogue. I have kept adding cool stuff to it; the tour below is the quick version.

The tour

  • 1 · The main page does what it says: pick a city in India (or all ten at once) and it shows you satellite visibility from that location, in real time. The right-hand panel splits the count into Chinese and non-Chinese satellites, a toggle can mask everything non-Chinese, and the Switch 2D-3D button flips between flat markers and true orbital altitudes. Click any satellite to see the ground it covered over the past 30 minutes and the direction it is heading.
  • 2 · SAT-STATS (the ▦ button on the right-hand panel) lists satellites from all over the world, sorted by country of origin — pick India from the dropdown and see exactly how many of our space vehicles are up there right now. Below the table sit graphs that give an instinctive overview of the satellite world: there are individual launch sites in the USA and China that have launched more rockets than the total number of Indian satellites in space.
  • 3 · The TLE Repository (the ⌗ button inside SAT-STATS) is the technical corner: the raw Two-Line Elements of every satellite vehicle tracked, with a layman's decoder underneath that explains what each character of a TLE means. It might be fun to try and understand one.
  • 4 · 2D-VIEW shows the same real-time positions on a flat 2-D world map — the easiest view to screenshot.
  • 5 · CHINA-SAT-REPO is my attempt at a sort of Wikipedia page: it untangles the names and nomenclature of Chinese satellites (Yaogan vs Gaofen vs Shiyan…) for a layman, with a deeper Compendium of every major PRC programme attached. It is meant for exploring.
  • 6 · GAME-OF-CONES is built for a custom purpose. In Land Cone you pick a lat/long and define a cone of visibility — the globe shows every satellite inside it, which makes it a handy tool for working out what sits at which angles when you look skyward from a particular location. It also has the inverse: Sat Cone visually displays a chosen satellite's look area on the ground, with the sensor angle fully adjustable.
  • 7 · 3D-VISUALISER gives you an instinctive God's-eye view of the entire catalogue — all ~16,000 satellites, at their true altitudes, in real time, all at once. Clicking an individual satellite dims everything else and draws its complete path in space — genuinely the coolest thing to watch here.
  • 8 · SATS-BY-OPS is the same God's-eye view, but the satellites are differentiated by operator: pick a Maxar, a Capella, an ICEYE — or all of Starlink, or the whole BeiDou constellation — and see exactly where their fleets are right now. Hover an operator's name and its satellites pulse on the globe.
  • 9 · Saved the quirkiest for last 😄 — PLAY NAZAR. Early attempts at tracing live orbits produced some unforeseen glitches that were, frankly, beautiful to look at. So I added a soundtrack: press the ♪ Play NAZAR button on the Orbit Visualisation page and the screen goes full-bleed while the whole globe gyrates to the same BPM as the track. An entirely not-useful feature — and the most fun one to build.

Under the hood

  • Orbits come from NORAD/CelesTrak TLE data and are computed in your browser with the standard SGP4 propagator — the same maths the space agencies use.
  • A scheduled robot (GitHub Actions) re-downloads the catalogue every 6 hours and ships it with the site, so everything keeps working even when the live feed rate-limits you.
  • Nothing about you is stored anywhere: the only data kept is satellite data, cached in your own browser for speed.
  • Want the full story? The two documents at the bottom of this page contain the complete annotated source code and a plain-language explainer.

Quick tips

  • Drag to rotate any globe, scroll / pinch to zoom, hover a dot for its details.
  • Click a satellite to isolate it (main page and 3D-Visualiser); click it again — or empty space — to release.
  • The Observer City dropdown on the main page also has All cities (union of all ten horizons) and a Mask non-Chinese toggle.
  • On mobile, everything collapses into the ☰ MENU button at the top-left.
